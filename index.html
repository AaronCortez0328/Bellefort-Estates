About

Bellefort Estates is a residential community on rolling ground in Barangay San Miguel, Molino IV, Bacoor. It was developed by Pro-Friends and opened in 2009 with the first linear park in Molino.

Community

Living in Bellefort.

01

Security & access

Guards on duty 24/7. The estate has three entrance gates and sits away from the main road. The Molino IV gate is the main entry today.

02

The linear park

A strip of green behind the homes, turned over with grass, trees, its own drainage, and a gate and fence. It was the first in Molino.

03

Facilities

The developer lists a clubhouse, a swimming pool and a basketball court among the estate's amenities, along with landscaped open spaces.

04

Utilities

A centralized water system serves the estate. The community is built on elevated, rolling ground next to a commercial and business park.

History

  1. 1999

    Pro-Friends begins operations. Property Company of Friends goes on to deliver more than 20,000 homes, mostly in Cavite.

  2. 2009

    Bellefort Estates launches. The 110-hectare estate opens in November with the Charlotte, Sabine and Vivienne models and Molino's first linear park.

  3. 2015

    GT Capital invests. GT Capital Holdings acquires a 22.68% stake in Pro-Friends.

  4. 2016

    Majority stake. GT Capital raises its economic stake in Pro-Friends to 51%.

  5. 2023

    Phase 3. New homes are completed in Phase 3, now offered under the Breighton Land brand.

Data Privacy

Your privacy matters.

Bellefort Estates Homeowners Association respects your privacy and recognizes the need for appropriate measures to protect and manage the personal data entrusted to us, in line with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173).

  • What we collect. Basic identity and contact details, your address in the estate, and records of dues, requests and gate access.
  • Why. To manage membership and accounts, process payments, keep the estate secure, and respond to your concerns.
  • Your rights. You may access, correct or ask us to erase your data, object to its processing, and file a complaint with the National Privacy Commission.

Data privacy and security: how access is limited

Voluntary participation. Private by default. Show a clear notice, verify only necessary details, let the homeowner choose listing fields, and let the Livelihood Committee review publication. Address and verification records stay private; external chat links follow the selected app's policies.

Use named accounts, privileged MFA, tested database/file permissions, minimal logs, encrypted backups and recovery drills. Provide correction, unpublish and privacy-request paths. Outsourcing pros

Ready-made functions, onboarding, vendor updates and support.

Trade-offs

Product-roadmap limits, subscription/usage charges, vendor-owned code and contract-dependent export/exit. Some hosted plans genuinely cost less than our core infrastructure.

Review findings: PayHOA GetApp: 4.7/5, 727 reviews; useful administration/support, with some banking, reporting and fee concerns. HOA Express Trustpilot: 4.7/5, 43 reviews, mostly positive and only two in the last year; a separate June 2026 review requested more flexible site organisation. Qmunidad's GetApp listing showed zero reviews; no verified HOAS user-review sample was established.

Our strongest edge

Donated development, locally tailored Livelihood workflows and ₱0 customisation labour within volunteer coverage. Choose using same-scope cost, privacy, continuity and ownership evidence. Three-year TCO, full fees and linked review details are in the Word guide.
