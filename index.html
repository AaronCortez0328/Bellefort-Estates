Executive Summary

Proposed portal · For HOA review

Bellefort HOA Portal Executive Summary

Bellefort needs one dependable place to find official updates, follow community requests and discover opportunities within the village. The proposed HOA portal brings these activities into a structured website, with access based on each person’s verified role.

View portal planPrivacy & security

Proposed outcome

What success should look like.

A homeowner can find the latest approved notice, submit a concern once, follow its progress and check an authorised record without relying on a personal connection.

The community

How the community benefits

01

For Homeowners and OFW owners

Read approved updates when convenient, follow your own requests, and see community project progress. Where later approved, access your own statements and receipts without asking someone to forward private records. An owner’s absence from the village should not mean an absence of information.

02

For Residents and Renters

Receive notices relevant to the residence, report common-area problems and use services permitted by the tenancy and HOA rules. A renter’s account is verified and linked to an authorised residence; it does not automatically carry a member’s governance rights.

03

For Committees and the Board

Maintain a clear queue of work, record decisions and identify unresolved issues. Approved summary reports help the Board review service performance without routinely browsing everyone’s private household information.

The proposed first phase

Start with the Livelihood program

A practical first phase is a community hub for verified, willing homeowners. It can help residents find local products, services and work opportunities while giving participating owners a clearer way to promote what they offer.

Category What homeowners can do

  • Business Directory

    Find participating businesses, products and professional services by category.

  • Marketplace Buy and Sell

    Post permitted items for sale and contact the listing owner directly.

  • Garage Sales

    Publish approved sale dates and listings so neighbours can plan ahead.

  • Booth Events

    Express interest in seasonal community events under published allocation rules.

  • Job Openings and Referrals

    Share legitimate vacancies, skills and referral opportunities without publishing applicants’ CVs.

  • Seminars and Workshop Training

    Learn practical skills for managing your property and lowering living costs.

Free participation with clear choices. Registration in this initial Livelihood program is proposed to be free. Homeowners choose the business information and contact links they want displayed to the approved audience. Listings may link to a business website, Facebook page, Messenger, Viber or WhatsApp.

How participation works

The Livelihood Committee will verify eligibility, review listings against published rules, record approval or rejection reasons, and handle corrections and reports. IT volunteers maintain the technology under approved access controls. Listings do not guarantee sales, employment or HOA endorsement.

Simple participation process. Express interest, review the privacy notice, verify eligibility, choose display fields, submit a listing, receive committee review, then publish. Owners can update or request removal of their listing; the committee keeps a proportionate audit trail.

Seminars and Workshop Training

Empower homeowners by providing the knowledge and practical skills needed to effectively manage their properties, lower living costs, build a safer, more cohesive neighborhood and opportunities for livelihood.

Why the portal

Why Bellefort needs the portal

Our current setup relies on many group chats, Facebook posts, separate forms and manual follow-ups. Important notices can get buried, records can become inconsistent, and residents may need to repeat the same concern to several people. Owners working overseas or living elsewhere can find it particularly difficult to stay informed.

The goal is to make community information easier to find and services easier to follow. Group chats can continue to notify and discuss; the portal should hold the approved notice, current record and request history.

Current difficulties and proposed improvements
Current difficulty Proposed improvement Benefit to homeowners
Updates scattered across chats One approved notice with an issuer, date and version Find the current information without searching many threads
Requests followed up by message Reference number, assigned committee and status history See who is handling a request and its next step
Separate files and repeated encoding Validated records and controlled updates Fewer conflicting versions and repeated submissions
Limited visibility for absent owners Mobile access to authorised records and updates Stay involved across locations and time zones
Business offers quickly disappear in feeds Searchable community listings Find and support participating neighbours

Why a community may be slow to adopt a portal

Possible barriers include limited funds, volunteer time, poor connectivity, unfamiliarity with technology, migration effort, privacy concerns and uncertainty over long-term support. These are problems to resolve through a phased pilot, training, clear costs and an assisted service route.

Poorly documented decisions, selective release of records, unexplained refusals and control concentrated in one person can weaken accountability. These are governance risks to examine against actual records and applicable rules. Resistance to a portal alone is not evidence of a hidden agenda, and we should not attribute improper motives to other HOAs without evidence.

Transparency that homeowners can check

Transparency improves when information has an accountable owner, an approved source and a visible history. A website by itself cannot ensure good governance. The portal must support the following practices.

Proposed transparency practices
Transparency feature What homeowners should be able to check
Approved notices and records Issuer, approval date, period covered and the current version of a notice or authorised record.
Request tracking Their own reference number, acknowledgement, assigned team, status, reasons for closure and route to question the outcome.
Project and financial summaries Approved scope, milestones, funding or sponsorship summaries, spending summaries and dates of the latest review.
Corrections and decision history Visible corrections to published records, with earlier versions preserved for authorised review.
Fair community participation Published listing and booth rules, reasons for decisions, conflict disclosures and a way to appeal.

Community transparency must protect household privacy. Publish approved, appropriately redacted information to the right audience. A neighbour’s balance, complaint, contact details or visitor history should not become a community feed item. Provide a proper records-request route where a member may lawfully be entitled to additional records.

A community asset that can outlast a Board term

Continuity requires written HOA rights to operate and maintain the portal, documented handover, two independent recovery custodians and a secure way to export records or change providers. Proposed pro bono support from Multiply Talents must have disclosed interests, independent approval and written terms. Ralph should recuse from decisions approving his company’s access, engagement or acceptance. Resident data must not be reused for marketing or political campaigning.

How these permissions must work

Operational scope. Guards access minimum details for the assigned gate or shift. Drivers access authorised rides and minimum dispatch details, without general household lookup. Private alerts go only to the relevant resident and assigned personnel. [7]

Governance scope. Homeowner material is approved, redacted and subject to entitlement. BOD reviews use summaries; restricted cases require assignments and recusal. R on technical tools means assurance reports, not keys or console access.

Technical scope. Super admin is proposed, not shown in the screenshots. T covers configuration and diagnostic metadata, without routine business-record views. Infrastructure credentials can bypass application restrictions; production support access therefore needs justification, expiry, independent approval and audit. [8]

Action controls. O excludes self-approval and self-verification of ownership or tenancy. Statements are read only; request corrections through a ticket. M covers assigned processing and drafts; publication needs another authorised reviewer. Deny bulk export, silent log editing and permanent deletion by default. Nobody grants their own privileged role.

Separate capacities. A director’s personal homeowner permissions remain distinct from BOD duties. Keep binding voting, proxies, collection, penalties and CCTV release disabled pending separate authority and review.

Full privacy requirements from the proposal

Privacy and security explained clearly

Your trust should rest on evidence. Our launch requirements include tested access controls, privacy processes and recovery. No online system is completely secure. Homeowners should be able to see what was tested, when it was reviewed and how to report a concern.

What protects your information

Verified accounts and limited access. The proposed design limits records by role and authorised residence, including database access, files, searches, downloads and notifications. A hidden menu alone is not protection.

Stronger protection for powerful accounts. Our requirements include multifactor authentication, named privileged accounts, prompt revocation, logged changes and independent approval for sensitive actions. Board membership or technical responsibility should not give routine access to every private record.

Protected storage and recovery. Launch requirements include encrypted connections, verified encryption at rest, private files, protected backups and tested restoration. The demo must use separate synthetic data. User training, updates and incident exercises support these controls. [2, 4, 5]

What homeowners should know about their data

Collect only what is needed. Identity and residence details support eligibility; contact details support service updates; business details support optional listings. Home addresses and private contacts stay hidden by default in the proposed design. The privacy notice must explain purposes, lawful bases, recipients and retention. [1]

Publication is a separate choice. Participation is voluntary, with separate, unticked choices for optional listing fields and contact links. Portal registration or information given through an earlier QR code, form or chat is not blanket permission for publication or a new use. [1, 4]

You retain privacy rights. The portal needs a working route for access, correction, applicable removal or blocking, and withdrawal of optional publication consent. The privacy notice explains retention, providers, overseas processing and the HOA’s privacy contact. [1]

BEHOA remains accountable for processing it determines. Activation requires an impact assessment, applicable NPC registration review and provider agreements. A provider badge or NPC registration certificate does not prove full portal compliance. [1, 2, 3, 6]
